Oregon DOJ initial charity registration guide

Oregon charitable organization registration

Oregon DOJ registration is a one-time charity record for covered corporations, associations, and trusts. The practical filing path is to choose RF-C or RF-T, assemble the governing documents and tax-status evidence the form requests, submit the package before covered activity begins, and calendar the first annual CT-12 or CT-12F report.

Oregon at a glance

  • Regulator: Oregon Department of Justice, Charitable Activities Section.
  • Corporation form: RF-C for Oregon or foreign corporations, associations, and similar entities.
  • Initial fee: The current RF-C instructions say to submit no fee with registration.
  • Ongoing report: CT-12 for Oregon charities or CT-12F for foreign corporations, generally due four months and 15 days after fiscal year end.
Scope note: This page covers the Oregon Department of Justice Charitable Activities Section. Oregon Secretary of State corporation filings, IRS tax exemption, tax registration, charitable gaming, and professional fundraising-firm duties are separate.

Decide whether Oregon DOJ registration applies to the organization

The Oregon DOJ says registration generally applies to an Oregon public benefit nonprofit corporation, a nonprofit corporation organized elsewhere that solicits, holds assets, or does business in Oregon, a trust holding Oregon assets for charitable beneficiaries, or an association organized for charitable purposes. Its registration page also identifies exclusions such as certain religious organizations, noncharitable Oregon mutual benefit nonprofits, and qualifying educational institutions with limited Oregon activity.

Do not reduce that test to a donation counter. Oregon’s RF-C instructions frame coverage around holding charitable assets, soliciting donations, or conducting charitable activity in the state. Document the entity form, state of organization, Oregon fundraising, Oregon property, and Oregon program activity. If the facts sit near an exclusion, use the DOJ’s charity-registration contact rather than describing the organization as exempt from a generic template.

Choose RF-C or RF-T and keep corporation filings separate

RF-C is the current form for an Oregon or foreign corporation, association, or similar entity. RF-T is the form for a trust. An Oregon corporation must also maintain its separate Corporation Division record with the Secretary of State, and a foreign corporation may have an authority-to-transact-business question. Those corporate filings do not replace the Attorney General’s charitable registration.

Record the organization’s exact legal name, EIN, physical and mailing address, website, accounting period, formation jurisdiction and date, Oregon county of primary operations, tax-status stage, officers and directors, compensation, related organizations, fundraising firms, and program description from approved source documents. Use “none” or “not applicable” where the form instructs; the RF-C instructions specifically caution against leaving lines blank.

Assemble only the attachments the current registration form requests

For a corporation or association, the current RF-C instructions request filed articles of incorporation or the signed establishing document, signed and dated bylaws, and the IRS determination letter if applicable. A trust uses its creating will or trust document and the IRS determination letter when applicable. If a required document is unavailable, the instructions call for an explanation rather than an unexplained omission.

The initial RF-C instructions say to submit no fee. That makes the attachment and identity review especially important: there is no payment receipt to serve as even a preliminary timestamp. Create a final PDF set with descriptive file names, confirm signatures and dates, and keep a copy of exactly what was submitted. Do not add donor lists or a Form 990 unless the current registration instructions request them.

Submit before covered activity and preserve the registration number

Oregon’s current registration page says email is the best submission route and also permits mail or fax using the contact information on the form. The RF-C instructions say covered organizations should register before conducting charitable activities, holding charitable assets, or soliciting contributions; an Oregon corporation should register promptly after incorporation.

Save the outgoing message or delivery record, the complete RF-C or RF-T package, any DOJ questions, and the issued registration number. If the state requests clarification, respond from the same controlled record rather than opening a duplicate submission. Once the record appears in Oregon’s public charity database, compare the legal name, entity type, address, fiscal year, and registration number to the organization’s file.

Calendar the first annual CT-12 or CT-12F immediately

Initial registration is a one-time event, but Oregon requires annual financial reporting after registration. The DOJ’s current annual-report page uses CT-12 for organizations incorporated or based in Oregon and CT-12F for corporations based outside Oregon. The report is due within four months and 15 days after fiscal year end; a calendar-year charity therefore works toward May 15. A six-month extension request must be made by the initial due date.

Set the first annual deadline as part of the initial-registration closeout. Keep the federal return, state-specific financial inputs, governing-document changes, fundraiser information, and portal access under one owner. This separation protects search intent too: the RF-C page answers the first-time Oregon registration question, while the existing Oregon renewal guide owns the CT-12 and CT-12F annual-report workflow.

Oregon registration FAQ

Who generally registers a charity with the Oregon DOJ?

The state lists Oregon public benefit nonprofits, foreign nonprofits that solicit, hold assets, or do business in Oregon, trusts holding Oregon assets for charitable beneficiaries, and charitable associations, subject to stated exclusions.

Which Oregon charity registration form applies?

Use RF-C for an Oregon or foreign corporation, association, or similar entity. A trust uses RF-T.

Is there an Oregon DOJ initial charity registration fee?

The current RF-C instructions say to submit no fee with the initial registration. Annual financial reports can carry fees later.

What attachments does Oregon RF-C request?

The current instructions request the filed articles or establishing document, signed and dated bylaws, and the IRS determination letter if applicable. An unavailable document should be explained.

Does Oregon Secretary of State incorporation complete DOJ charity registration?

No. Corporation Division filings and the Attorney General’s charitable registration are separate records.

What happens after Oregon charity registration?

Calendar the annual CT-12 or CT-12F report, generally due four months and 15 days after fiscal year end, and preserve the DOJ registration number and public record.
