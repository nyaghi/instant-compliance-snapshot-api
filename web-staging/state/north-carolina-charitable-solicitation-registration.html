North Carolina initial charity licensing guide

North Carolina charitable solicitation registration

A nonexempt organization generally needs an approved North Carolina charitable solicitation license before it asks the public for contributions in the state. The filing work starts with the current $50,000 exemption test, then moves through the CSL application, financial record, fee, notarized certification, and public-license check.

North Carolina at a glance

  • Regulator: North Carolina Secretary of State, Charitable Solicitation Licensing Division.
  • Trigger: Unless exempt, obtain the license before soliciting contributions in North Carolina.
  • Current small-charity test: Less than $50,000 in calendar-year contributions and no compensation to the listed insiders.
  • Certification: Treasurer or chief fiscal officer signs under oath; online filing requires the notarized signature page upload.
Scope note: This page covers the North Carolina Secretary of State Charitable Solicitation Licensing record. Corporate authority, tax exemption, raffles, professional fundraisers, local permits, and another state’s registration are separate questions. It is informational and not legal or tax advice.

Resolve the current exemption test before opening the application

North Carolina General Statute 131F-3 now exempts a person that receives less than $50,000 in contributions in a calendar year and does not compensate any officer, trustee, organizer, incorporator, fundraiser, or solicitor. The statute lists several ways to demonstrate the revenue side of that test, including a filed Form 990 or 990-EZ, a 990-N submission confirmation, a board-approved current-year budget, or the Department’s financial form.

The Secretary of State’s forms library remains the right place to obtain the current filing route, but the downloadable 2020 application PDF still prints an older $25,000 exemption figure. Do not carry that legacy figure into the legal analysis. Use the current statute and the live agency FAQ, document the compensation facts, and ask CSL about any form-field mismatch before certifying the application. An exemption is fact-specific; it is not a blanket statement that every small nonprofit is outside Chapter 131F.

Build the initial CSL packet from the organization’s approved records

Section 131F-6 and the state application call for the organization’s legal and solicitation names, charitable purpose, use of contributions, principal and North Carolina locations, fiscal year end, programs, leadership, solicitation responsibility, custody and distribution of contributions, and regulatory history. A foreign corporation should also follow the application’s current evidence rule for proving legal existence in its home jurisdiction.

Create one source worksheet before entering the portal. Reconcile the legal name and state of formation to the corporate record; the tax status to the IRS determination; officer and director names to the current board roster; program and use-of-funds answers to approved campaign materials; and fundraising-firm answers to signed contracts. If a fundraising consultant, solicitor, or coventurer is involved, follow the application’s separate disclosure-form instructions instead of summarizing the relationship from memory.

Use the correct financial record for the immediately preceding year

The application asks for at least one accepted financial record for the immediately preceding fiscal year: a signed Form 990 or 990-EZ with Schedule A, an audited financial statement, or the North Carolina Annual Financial Report form. The state application says a Form 990-N postcard by itself does not satisfy this license-application financial-information requirement. A newly established applicant without financial history uses a current-year budget with projected revenue and expenses.

That is different from using a 990-N confirmation to demonstrate the revenue component of the current statutory exemption. Keep those two decisions separate. First determine whether the organization is exempt. If it is applying for a license, then use the financial attachment the application accepts. Remove no requested public material silently, and review sensitive schedules against the live instructions before upload because filed license records can be available through the public registry.

Calculate the statutory fee and complete the notarized filing

For an organization that must be licensed, current section 131F-8 sets the ordinary application fee at $50 when prior-year contributions are below $100,000, $100 when they are at least $100,000 but below $200,000, and $200 at $200,000 or more. A licensed filer below $5,000 pays no fee, and a consolidated parent filing has a separate chapter-based schedule. Use the statute and the live portal calculation; do not use the stale exemption amount printed on the older PDF as the fee authority.

The application must be signed under oath by the treasurer or chief fiscal officer. The online-filing page requires a completed, signed, and notarized signature form to be uploaded. Save the completed application, every attachment, the notarized page, payment evidence, submission confirmation, and any deficiency notice. A draft saved in the portal is not proof that the Department received a complete application.

Add the solicitation disclosure and verify the public license

Licensing does not end the operational work. Section 131F-9 requires specified disclosures at the point of solicitation and a conspicuous state notice on printed solicitations, written confirmations, receipts, or contribution reminders for licensed organizations. Use the statutory wording and the current agency contact information rather than paraphrasing the disclosure into a weaker marketing sentence.

After approval, search the Secretary of State registry for the organization’s exact legal name and confirm the license number, filed materials, and status signal. Save the dated result with the submission evidence and calendar the annual renewal from the organization’s fiscal-year and federal-return controls. North Carolina registration FAQ

When must a nonprofit obtain a North Carolina charitable solicitation license?

Unless an exemption applies, section 131F-5 requires the organization to obtain the license before soliciting contributions in North Carolina, having contributions solicited on its behalf, or participating in a covered charitable sales promotion.

What is North Carolina’s current small-charity exemption threshold?

Current section 131F-3 uses less than $50,000 in calendar-year contributions together with no compensation to the officers, trustees, organizers, incorporators, fundraisers, or solicitors named in the statute.

Why does an older North Carolina application show $25,000?

The downloadable application PDF is dated 2020 and still prints the earlier amount. The current statute and live agency FAQ use $50,000, so a filer should apply current law and resolve any legacy form mismatch with CSL before certifying.

Can a Form 990-N be the financial attachment for a license application?

The current application says the 990-N postcard is not sufficient for the application’s financial-information requirement. It can be evidence for the revenue side of the statutory exemption test, which is a different decision.

Who signs the North Carolina charity license application?

Section 131F-6 calls for the treasurer or chief fiscal officer to sign under oath. The online route also requires the signed and notarized signature page upload.

How should a charity confirm North Carolina approval?

Save the state submission and acceptance evidence, then search the Secretary of State registry for the correct legal entity and license record. A payment receipt alone is not an approval record.
