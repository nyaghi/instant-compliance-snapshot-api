Oregon DOJ delinquent charity report recovery guide

Oregon charity delinquency recovery

Oregon charity delinquency is usually a year-by-year reporting problem. A reliable cure identifies every missing CT-12, CT-12F, or CT-12S period, calculates the revenue, net-asset, and automatic late fees for each report, supplies the federal-return attachments, and verifies the updated Oregon DOJ record.

Oregon at a glance

  • Regulator: Oregon Department of Justice, Charitable Activities Section.
  • Annual forms: CT-12 for Oregon charities; CT-12F for foreign corporations; CT-12S for split-interest trusts.
  • Ordinary due date: Four months and 15 days after fiscal year end unless a timely Oregon extension applies.
  • Published late tiers: $20 initially, $50 after 13 months, and $100 after 16 months from fiscal year end.
Scope note: This page covers annual reporting to the Oregon Department of Justice Charitable Activities Section. Oregon Secretary of State corporation filings, federal tax returns, charitable gaming, and professional fundraiser records are separate. It does not promise that filing automatically lifts an agency order.

Map the public record to each missing reporting period

Oregon requires registered charitable organizations to file an annual written report. The form depends on the entity: CT-12 for an organization incorporated, organized, or headquartered in Oregon; CT-12F for a corporation organized and headquartered outside Oregon; and CT-12S for a split-interest trust. Begin with the DOJ charity database and the organization’s prior reports. Record the Oregon registration number, legal name, fiscal year, displayed status, latest report on file, and every missing period.

Do not treat one current-year filing as a cure for a multi-year gap. Use a separate row for every fiscal period showing the correct form year, form type, federal return, audit status, revenue fee, net-asset fee, late fee, signature, and submission status. Oregon says the form year must match the year in which the fiscal period began, which matters when rebuilding older reports from a current forms page.

Build complete CT-12-family reports instead of placeholders

The current CT-12 instructions require the report, all schedules and attachments, and payment. The report covers organization information, audit status, fundraising-firm contracts, relevant legal or administrative actions, governing-document and tax-status changes, responsible persons, financial amounts, and the officer signature. A report can be treated as incomplete when required lines are blank or when zero revenue or net assets lacks an explanation.

Attach the complete federal return and supporting schedules filed with the IRS, subject to Oregon’s stated Schedule B treatment. A 990-N filer should include the postcard confirmation when available. The instructions also warn that some organizations with at least $50,000 of revenue or more than $100,000 of assets may need to prepare a 990-EZ or 990 for Oregon purposes even if the form was not federally required. Resolve that question before submission rather than sending an obviously partial packet.

Apply the Oregon deadline and late-fee rules period by period

ORS 128.670 and the DOJ annual-report page use a due date of four months and 15 days after fiscal year end. A calendar-year organization therefore has a May 15 ordinary due date. An Oregon extension request must be received by the initial due date; an IRS or Department of Revenue extension does not extend the DOJ report unless a copy is timely submitted to the Charitable Activities Section. The current instructions allow a maximum 180-day extension.

The 2025 CT-12 instructions publish an automatic late fee of $20 when the report or fee misses the due or extended date. The amount increases to $50 if the item remains outstanding more than 13 months after the end of the fiscal year and to $100 if the delinquency is not corrected within 16 months of fiscal year end. The instructions say these late fees cannot be waived. Calculate the late tier independently for every open year and add the report’s revenue and net-asset fees.

Treat enforcement status as a separate recovery question

ORS 128.670 permits the Attorney General, after the statutory notice process, to impose a civil penalty of up to $2,000 when a delinquent report or required fee remains unresolved within 90 days after notice. The Attorney General may also order an organization to cease soliciting until required reports and fees are filed or paid. ORS 128.675 separately authorizes denial, conditions, suspension, or revocation of registration for covered violations.

That means a complete late report and payment are necessary cure steps, but they should not be described as automatically ending a specific order, penalty, suspension, or revocation. Save every notice and identify its response deadline and requested evidence. If an order exists, follow the agency’s instructions for the organization’s record and preserve the written confirmation that the matter has been resolved.

File through the current channel and verify acceptance

Oregon’s current annual-report page offers an online portal for reports and credit-card payment and also provides the forms and mailing address. Use unsecured PDFs for portal attachments, name files by year and document type, and keep the registration number on every report and payment record. For mailed filings, the instructions state that receipt—not the postmark—controls timely filing, so retain delivery evidence without treating it as agency acceptance.

After filing, save the full report, attachments, officer signature, fee workpaper, payment confirmation, correspondence, and the dated public database result. Confirm that each missing period now appears and that any enforcement notation has been addressed. Oregon recovery FAQ

Which report is used to cure an Oregon charity delinquency?

Use CT-12 for an Oregon-based charity, CT-12F for a corporation organized and headquartered outside Oregon, or CT-12S for a split-interest trust, with the form year matching the reporting period.

What is the Oregon charity annual-report due date?

The ordinary deadline is four months and 15 days after fiscal year end. A state extension request must be received by the initial due date.

How much is the Oregon CT-12 late fee?

Current instructions use $20 initially, $50 when the item remains outstanding more than 13 months after fiscal year end, and $100 if not corrected within 16 months.

Can Oregon waive a CT-12 late fee?

The current CT-12 instructions state that the published late fees apply automatically and cannot be waived.

Does filing a delinquent Oregon report automatically lift a cease-soliciting order?

Do not assume so. File and pay what is required, then follow the notice or order and obtain written agency confirmation for that specific record.

How should an organization prove Oregon recovery?

Keep every report, attachment, signature, fee calculation, payment record, and agency response, then verify that the DOJ public record reflects the corrected reporting history and status.
