Colorado annual charitable registration renewal guide

Colorado charity registration renewal

Colorado renewal follows the organization’s fiscal year and federal-return timing, not a universal calendar date. The filing must pair the current registration record with the most recent financial report, a properly documented Colorado extension when needed, consistent Form 990 information, and proof that the Secretary of State returned the registration to good status.

Colorado at a glance

  • Ordinary deadline: The 15th day of the fifth month after fiscal year end when no IRS extension is filed.
  • IRS-extension deadline: The 15th day of the eighth month after fiscal year end under the current rule.
  • Extra state extension: A further three months can be requested through the Colorado charities site when eligible.
  • Overdue charity fine: Current rules assess $60 per overdue renewal or estimated-financial update.
Scope note: This page covers annual Colorado charitable organization registration renewal. Initial registration, reinstatement, corporate periodic reports, federal Form 990 filing, paid-solicitor and fundraising-consultant renewals, and another state’s record are separate. Calculate the Colorado date from the fiscal year and IRS extension

Start with the accounting period used in the organization’s books and federal return. Current Colorado rules require renewal and the financial report by the fifteenth day of the fifth calendar month after fiscal year end when the organization did not file an IRS extension. When it did file a federal extension, the Colorado rule uses the fifteenth day of the eighth month. For a calendar-year charity, those checkpoints are May 15 and August 15.

The public certificate should show the operative renewal or actual-financial deadline, including an extension. Save it with the prior filing, fiscal-year workpaper, Form 990 status, and any state notice. Do not borrow a date from a corporate periodic report, a paid solicitor’s anniversary, or another state. Colorado also keeps an electronic filing deadline on a weekend or legal holiday on that day, so do not assume the next-business-day rule applies.

Request the Colorado extension instead of relying on Form 8868 alone

An IRS extension can support the Colorado timetable, but current state rules expressly say filing Form 8868 with the IRS or sending a copy to Colorado is not enough to extend the state deadline. File the required extension action through the Colorado charities registration website on or before the applicable deadline. The Secretary of State may ask for Form 8868 or an IRS confirmation as evidence of the federal extension.

If an organization with an IRS extension still cannot file by the fifteenth day of the eighth month, the rules allow a request for an additional three-month Colorado extension through the charities site. Preserve the request, supporting reason, federal evidence, state confirmation, and revised certificate date. A late extension request does not erase an already accrued filing problem.

Reconcile the renewal to the most recent financial period

Prepare the legal and solicitation names, addresses, responsible people, officers and directors, tax status, fundraising relationships, and the financial report for the most recently completed fiscal year. Reconcile revenue, contributions, expenses, program activity, fundraising costs, fiscal period, and vendor relationships to the federal return and accounting records. Colorado can request the appropriate Form 990 and require correction of state-federal inconsistencies.

An organization that used estimated data on initial registration must replace it with actual information by the applicable fifth- or eighth-month deadline. If initial registration used financial information outside the most recently completed fiscal year, current rules can require a renewal or extension within five days after approval. Keep those estimate-conversion tasks separate from the ordinary annual renewal so neither one is overlooked.

Control delinquency, fines, and suspension timing

Colorado changes the registration status to delinquent the day after a missed deadline. Current rules assess a $60 fine for an overdue charity renewal or overdue update of estimated financial information. A nonexempt charity that continues to solicit after state notice while unregistered can face an additional fine and reporting duties for earlier solicitation periods. Treat those charges separately from ordinary filing fees.

Current rules also provide a suspension path when an overdue renewal or hearing request is not received by the stated post-deadline period. A suspended organization may not solicit contributions in Colorado. Do not wait for the status to escalate before assembling the return, extension evidence, financial report, and payment. Fine-waiver standards are narrow and time limited; they are not a routine substitute for a timely filing.

Define completion by the updated certificate and public status

File through the existing Colorado Charities and Fundraisers account, use the correct organization record, retain the full filing and payment evidence, and monitor any deficiency. Update material name, address, leadership, entity-form, tax-status, or business changes through the appropriate amendment route rather than forcing every change into an annual filing without review.

After approval, download the new certificate and confirm that the public record shows good status and the next deadline. Save the approved filing, certificate, Form 990 support, state extension proof, deficiency responses, and receipt in a year-labeled folder. Calendar the fifth-month deadline, the possible eighth-month checkpoint, and the Colorado extension decision date before the next fiscal year closes.

Colorado renewal FAQ

When is Colorado charity registration renewal due?

Without an IRS extension, current rules use the fifteenth day of the fifth month after fiscal year end. With a federal extension, they use the fifteenth day of the eighth month.

Does filing IRS Form 8868 automatically extend Colorado renewal?

No. Current Colorado rules say the federal extension or a copy of Form 8868 is not by itself sufficient; the organization must complete the Colorado extension action on time.

Can Colorado grant more time after the eighth-month deadline?

An eligible organization with an IRS extension may request an additional three-month extension through the Colorado charities website before the applicable deadline.

What is the current Colorado overdue charity renewal fine?

The current rules assess $60 per overdue charity renewal or overdue update of estimated financial information, apart from other possible fees or fines.

What happens the day after a Colorado renewal deadline?

The current rules say the charity registration status changes to delinquent the day after a missed filing deadline.

When is Colorado charity renewal complete?

Use the approved renewal, updated Secretary of State certificate, and public record showing good status and the next deadline—not only a portal submission or payment.
