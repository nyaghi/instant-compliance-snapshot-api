Washington late, closed, or involuntarily closed charity recovery guide

Washington charity registration recovery

Washington recovery begins with the CCFS record and the organization’s fiscal-year deadline. A late but open charity may need the annual renewal and late fee, while a voluntarily or involuntarily closed charity uses re-registration. A record closed beyond six years is routed through registration. The cure must also address incomplete information, payment, and current solicitation activity.

Washington at a glance

  • Annual due date: The last business day of the eleventh month after fiscal year end.
  • Late renewal fee: $50 in addition to the $40 annual renewal fee.
  • Re-registration fee: $60 under the current state fee schedule.
  • Closed over six years: The current forms page routes the record through a new registration.
Scope note: This page covers Washington Charities Program registration recovery under chapter 19.09 RCW. Nonprofit corporation reinstatement, charitable trust filings, federal tax status, commercial fundraiser recovery, and another state’s record are separate. Diagnose the CCFS charity record and the corporation separately

Search CCFS by the legal name and UBI and save the charity record, status, fiscal year, expiration or renewal date, prior filings, correspondence, and organization number. Then save the separate business-entity record. A delinquent or dissolved nonprofit corporation and a late or closed charity registration are different problems; repairing one does not repair the other.

Build a filing timeline showing the last accepted charity renewal, accounting year, federal return, financial report, notices, changes, fundraising contracts, payments, and public solicitation activity. Identify whether the charity record is open and late, voluntarily closed, involuntarily closed, optional, or closed beyond six years. The current state forms page assigns different CCFS transactions to those conditions.

Choose annual renewal, re-registration, or initial registration

A charity renewal is due annually by the end of the eleventh month after fiscal year end. Use that route for an existing open record that needs its annual update. The state describes re-registration for a charity, optional charity, or commercial fundraiser that became voluntarily or involuntarily closed. If the organization has never been registered or its record has been closed or involuntarily closed beyond six years, the forms page routes it through registration.

Do not create a duplicate organization because a former staff member controlled the old CCFS account. Washington says user accounts are not tied to a specific business or organization. A new authorized user can create an account and work with the existing record. Preserve the record match and contact the Charities Program when names, UBIs, or prior accounts create an ambiguous identity.

Rebuild the financial and governance information

Prepare the organization and solicitation names, addresses, responsible people, highest-compensated officers or employees, purpose, federal tax status, financial-statement preparer, commercial fundraiser, and solicitation report. The financial report covers the preceding completed accounting year, including solicitation types, gross revenue, contributions, charitable-purpose spending, and total expenses.

The governing body or its committee must review and accept the financial report, and the president, treasurer, or comparable officer signs the application. Reconcile the Washington figures to the accounting records and federal return even when a Form 990 copy is not uploaded. Report specified changes within 30 days. A mismatched fiscal year or incomplete financial report can keep the cure from becoming complete.

Calculate the filing and late charges by transaction

The current fee schedule lists $40 for annual charity renewal, $60 for re-registration, and a $50 late fee for failure to renew by the due date. Do not combine those amounts without confirming the actual transaction in CCFS. Preserve the fee calculation and payment receipt, and resolve any earlier missing-fee notice rather than paying against a new duplicate record.

Washington may hold an incomplete application or missing-fee filing for up to 30 days while requesting a cure. If complete information or payment is not supplied, the applicant is deemed unregistered and must cease solicitations. Treat a deficiency deadline as an operational stop point: assign an owner, reconcile the response, and preserve the state confirmation that the missing item was accepted.

Control fundraising and verify the restored status

Inventory public donation pages, Washington-targeted campaigns, events, peer-to-peer activity, grants, and commercial fundraiser relationships while the charity status is not current. Chapter 19.09 ties the ability to solicit to registration and completeness. A submitted renewal or re-registration does not by itself prove the organization can resume activity; obtain record-specific guidance when timing matters.

The statute says entities are registered no sooner than 20 days after receipt of the registration or renewal, subject to the incomplete-information and payment rules. Verify the official CCFS charity record, legal name, UBI, status, and next renewal date, then save the approved filing and confirmation. Calendar the next last-business-day-of-the-eleventh-month deadline and the board review of the solicitation report.

Washington recovery FAQ

What is the first step for a late Washington charity registration?

Save the CCFS charity record, fiscal year, due date, status, notices, and prior filings, and keep the separate nonprofit corporation record in the same workpaper.

When does a Washington charity use re-registration?

The current state forms page uses re-registration when a charity has become voluntarily or involuntarily closed. A record closed beyond six years is routed through registration.

What are the Washington charity renewal and late fees?

The current fee schedule lists a $40 annual renewal fee and a separate $50 late fee for missing the renewal deadline.

What is the Washington charity re-registration fee?

The current state fee schedule lists $60 for charitable organization re-registration.

Can a new CCFS user recover an existing charity record?

Washington says user accounts are not tied to a specific organization, so a new authorized user can create an account rather than creating a duplicate charity record.

When is Washington charity recovery complete?

Verify the official CCFS charity record shows the restored status and next deadline after the complete filing and payment are accepted; a submission receipt alone is not enough.
