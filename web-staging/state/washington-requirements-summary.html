Washington charity registration requirements for nonprofits

Washington generally applies its Charitable Solicitations Act to organizations that ask for or collect contributions from the public in the name of charity. Charity registration is separate from forming a nonprofit corporation and from obtaining federal tax-exempt status.

Washington registration decision path

Washington's nonprofit-corporation filing, federal tax status, and charity registration are different records. A nonprofit should not use an active corporate record or IRS determination letter as a substitute for a Charities Program decision.

1. Identify the public fundraising activity

Record how the organization asks for or collects contributions from Washington residents, including website appeals, email campaigns, events, and third-party fundraising arrangements.

2. Apply the exemption as a two-part test

The Secretary of State describes an exemption for an organization raising less than $50,000 from the public in an accounting year only when all activities, including fundraising, are carried out by unpaid people. A paid employee or contractor can change that analysis even when receipts are below the dollar threshold.

3. Choose the correct online service

The current forms page distinguishes registration, annual renewal, re-registration after closure, amendment, and closure. Online submission is generally mandatory; use the service that matches the public record instead of creating a duplicate filing.

4. Track the annual clock

The Charities Program says renewal is due by the end of the 11th month after the fiscal year ends. The filing can be submitted after the required financial information is finalized. Keep this deadline separate from the nonprofit corporation's annual report.

5. Verify the public record and fundraising language

Use the Charity/Fundraiser/Trust search to confirm registration status, renewal date, and filing history after staff review. Separately review Washington's disclosure requirements for each request for a contribution; registration should never be presented as state endorsement.

Easy-to-miss control: Washington says amendments are used for changes to previously recorded information, and its forms page directs organizations to report covered changes within 30 days under WAC 434-120-045.

Washington charity registration FAQ

When should a nonprofit use the Washington requirements summary?

Use it when you need a fast read on Washington Secretary of State filing authority, CCFS renewal timing, exemptions, audit or review thresholds, required documents, and recovery risks before deciding the next filing step.

What is the next step if Washington status is uncertain?

Start with a CharityClarity Aurora status check, then compare the result against the Washington registration, renewal, or delinquency guide depending on whether the issue is first-time registration, annual filing, or recovery.

Does the Washington summary replace the full state guide?

No. The summary is a fast operational reference. Use the full Washington registration guide when you need fundraising-trigger analysis, CCFS portal planning, or a broader multi-state registration decision.

Why does Washington renewal timing matter?

Washington charity renewals are fiscal-year based, and missed CCFS filings, missing financial attachments, or fundraiser-contract issues can create public status and solicitation-risk problems.

What is Washington's $50,000 charity registration exemption?

The Secretary of State describes an exemption for an organization raising less than $50,000 from the public in an accounting year when all activities, including fundraising, are carried out by unpaid people. Both parts of the test matter.

Is Washington charity registration the same as the nonprofit corporation annual report?

No. The Charities Program registration and renewal record is separate from the business-entity record. A nonprofit may need to maintain both, with different deadlines and filing services.
