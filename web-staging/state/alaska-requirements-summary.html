Alaska charitable solicitation registration requirements for nonprofits

Alaska generally requires a charitable organization to register with the Department of Law before soliciting contributions in the state. Registration is filed online, costs $40, and expires every September 1 regardless of the original filing date.

This Alaska charity registration guide explains the filing authority, annual deadline, limited exemptions, required information, and the recovery path after a lapse. Confirm current instructions with the state before filing.

Requirement Alaska (AK)
Filing Authority Alaska Department of Law, Consumer Protection Unit (Attorney General).
Filing Method Online filing is required unless the Department waives it because the filer lacks reasonable internet or equipment access. The charitable organization filing fee is $40.
Filing Calendar & Triggers Initial registration is required before soliciting in Alaska. Every registration expires September 1; renewal is due by September 1 each year, and the Department does not grant extensions.
Filing Due Date September 1 each year, regardless of fiscal year-end. Alaska charges no late fee, but a required organization cannot solicit unless its registration is current.
Renewal Detail Annual renewal (Charitable Org Annual Registration form). No attachments required unless requested (simply pay $40 fee and confirm prior info).
Extensions Calendar None. The Department does not grant extensions; an incomplete Form 990 does not postpone the September 1 deadline.
Exemptions Limited exemptions include certain churches and religious organizations, specified political organizations, Alaska gaming-permit holders, and all-volunteer charities that stay within the state’s $5,000 and 10-person contribution limits. An exempt organization can file an online Notice of Exemption.
Good Standing Required for Charity Filing? Charitable solicitation registration is separate from nonprofit corporation qualification and business licensing. A foreign nonprofit should review those separate Alaska requirements based on its activities in the state.
Solicitation while Curing Deficiencies? No. A required organization may not solicit in Alaska without a current registration. A late filing becomes effective only after the registration is complete and approved.
Enforcement Posture Moderate. Alaska enforces registration through civil penalties (violations of the Charitable Solicitations Act can incur fines), but late filings incur no fees. Generally compliant-based enforcement.
Recovery Method File the annual registration after a lapse. Alaska does not charge a late fee, but the organization is not registered until the filing is received and approved and may not solicit while unregistered. Prior unregistered solicitation may still create enforcement exposure.
Required Documents No attachments are required with the online application. Do not submit the IRS determination letter, articles, bylaws, Form 990, or audited financial statements unless the Department requests them.
Financial Audit/Review Thresholds None by statute. No audit or CPA review is mandated for Alaska charitable filings (unless already prepared). However, orgs receiving significant state funds may need separate audits.
Schedule B Reaction Rule (Donor Names) Remove. Alaska does not publicly disclose Schedule B donor info. Charities are not required to submit donor names to the state (990 Schedule B not needed).
Signature Requirements The online registration is completed and certified electronically by an authorized filer.
High-Risk Traps / Enforcement Notes Fixed deadline: September 1 is not tied to fiscal year-end. Registration opens July 1 for the next solicitation period. A required organization cannot solicit until its registration is approved, and failure to register can lead to civil penalties under Alaska consumer-protection law.

Alaska charity registration FAQ

When must a nonprofit register to solicit in Alaska?

A charitable organization that is not exempt generally must register with the Alaska Department of Law before soliciting contributions of money or property in Alaska.

When is Alaska charity registration renewal due?

All registrations expire September 1 each year. Renewal is due by September 1, regardless of the organization’s fiscal year, and the Department does not grant extensions.

What does Alaska require with the online charity filing?

The organization pays the $40 fee and completes the online registration form. The Department says attachments such as the IRS determination letter, Form 990, audited financial statements, articles, and bylaws are not required unless requested.

Can a charity keep fundraising after missing September 1?

No. There is no late fee, but a required organization is not registered until its filing is received and approved and cannot solicit while unregistered.
