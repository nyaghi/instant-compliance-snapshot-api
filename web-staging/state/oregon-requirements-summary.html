Oregon DOJ filing decision points

Confirm the right Oregon report

Oregon DOJ annual reporting can point domestic charities toward CT-12 and out-of-state charities toward CT-12F. Match the form to the organization, filing period, attachments, and registration status before fundraising resumes.

Calendar the received-by deadline

The common Oregon due-date rule is 4 months and 15 days after fiscal year end. Extension requests need to be received by the original due date, so the practical risk is missing the DOJ deadline before the organization notices the lapse.

Prepare public-record attachments

Treat Oregon charity filings as public-facing records. Review IRS return attachments, financial statements, donor information handling, fundraiser contracts, and officer signature authority before submitting.

Route recovery before outreach

If the charity is delinquent or not current, triage back-year CT-12 filings, late fees, DOJ deficiency notices, and whether fundraising should pause until the public status is clean.

Why does Oregon annual report timing matter?

Oregon annual reports are generally due 4 months and 15 days after fiscal year end, and late or missing CT-12 filings, public-record attachments, or unresolved DOJ deficiencies can create public status problems.

What should an Oregon nonprofit check before filing CT-12 or CT-12F?

Check the correct Oregon DOJ report type, fiscal period, required IRS return or financial attachments, extension status, public-record redactions, fundraiser contracts, late fees, and whether any prior-year deficiency needs to be cured first.
