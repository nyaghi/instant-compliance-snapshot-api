Alaska September 1 annual registration guide

Alaska charity registration renewal

Every Alaska charitable organization registration expires September 1. The Department of Law opens the next solicitation-period filing July 1, charges $40, uses the organization’s FEIN and prior online confirmation number for renewal, grants no registration extension, and says no 990, audit, governing document, or paid-solicitor contract should be uploaded unless requested.

Alaska at a glance

  • Regulator: Alaska Department of Law, Consumer Protection Unit.
  • Annual deadline: September 1 for every charitable organization registration.
  • Filing opens: July 1 for the next solicitation period.
  • Registration fee: $40 before the online registration link is issued.
Scope note: This page covers the Alaska Department of Law charitable organization annual registration. Alaska corporation filings, gaming permits, IRS returns, and paid-solicitor registration are separate. The exemption analysis depends on the organization’s facts.

Treat September 1 as a fixed solicitation-registration boundary

The Alaska Department of Law states that all charitable organization registrations expire September 1, regardless of the organization’s fiscal year or the day it originally filed. Registration for the next solicitation period opens July 1. Put the fixed state date on the compliance calendar independently from the federal Form 990 and the Alaska corporate biennial report.

A filer that submits after September 1 does not incur a Department of Law late fee, but it is not registered until the filing is complete and approved. The state says an organization required to register cannot solicit contributions in Alaska while unregistered. That makes the no-late-fee rule a poor reason to wait; it is a pricing fact, not a grace period for fundraising.

Confirm exemption facts before paying the annual fee

The Department lists several exemptions, including certain religious organizations, political committees, current Alaska charitable gaming permit holders, and a narrow small-organization test. The small-organization route applies to an organization with no paid employees or board members that does not intend to raise or receive more than $5,000 annually, excluding government grants, or does not intend to receive contributions from more than 10 persons annually.

Read the live state wording and document both the compensation and fundraising facts before choosing an exemption. An organization using an exemption can submit the state’s online Notice of Exemption; the Department says that notice is submitted once. A current gaming permit is a separate record and should be verified rather than assumed from a prior event.

Use the prior confirmation number to enter the renewal record

The annual workflow begins with the $40 payment. The payment email supplies the link to continue the online charitable organization registration. A returning online filer enters its FEIN and the prior year’s confirmation number; the state says the renewal record will be prefilled so the filer can update the organization information.

Retrieve the confirmation number before the filing window becomes urgent. The Department says it appears on the prior confirmation screen and email and is also included in the next renewal reminder. Review every prefilled answer against current officers, addresses, financial information, fundraising methods, and paid-solicitor relationships. Prefill saves typing; it does not certify that last year’s facts remain accurate.

Follow Alaska’s no-attachment and no-extension rules

Alaska’s current FAQ says a charitable organization does not attach an IRS Form 990, audited financial statement, paid-solicitor contracts, IRS determination letter, articles, or bylaws to the registration unless the Department later requests material. Enter the information required by the online form and use the most current and complete financial information available.

The Department does not grant an extension for the charitable organization registration. An IRS extension can be reported in the Alaska form, but it does not move September 1. The state expressly says a charity can file even when the Form 990 is not complete. Do not delay the Alaska filing to wait for a federal return the state does not require as an attachment.

Save approval proof and understand what a late filing cannot repair

Save the payment receipt, filing link, completed answers, confirmation screen and email, confirmation number, and approval record. Then use the Alaska charity registration-status search to verify the organization’s public registration for the current solicitation period. A $40 payment is not proof that the online form was completed or approved.

The Department says it no longer accepts registrations for prior years. A late filing becomes effective when the current registration is complete; it does not backfill a missed historical period. If the public result or approval record is unclear, use the Department’s charity contact with the legal name and FEIN. After verification, save the dated public result and set the next internal preparation date before July 1.

Alaska renewal FAQ

When is Alaska charity registration renewal due?

Every Alaska charitable organization registration expires September 1. The next solicitation-period filing opens July 1.

What is the Alaska charity registration fee?

The Department of Law’s current filing page lists a $40 charitable organization registration payment.

Does Alaska grant an extension for charity registration?

No. The Department says it cannot extend the registration deadline. An IRS extension can be reported but does not move September 1.

What documents are attached to an Alaska charity renewal?

The current state FAQ says no 990, audit, governing documents, determination letter, or paid-solicitor contracts are attached unless the Department requests them.

Does Alaska charge a late registration fee?

No, but a required organization is not registered until its late filing is complete and approved and cannot solicit while unregistered.

Can a charity register for a missed prior Alaska year?

The Department says it no longer accepts registrations for prior years. A current late filing becomes effective when complete and does not backfill the missed period.
