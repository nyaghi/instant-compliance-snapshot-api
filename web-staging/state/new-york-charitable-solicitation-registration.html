New York Charitable Solicitation Registration

New York nonprofits that solicit donations (including online fundraising) may need to register before asking residents for contributions. This guide covers charitable solicitation registration New York and New York nonprofit registration fundraising in plain English.

New York registration runs through the Attorney General's Charities Bureau

The New York Attorney General says most organizations that hold charitable assets, engage in charitable activities in New York, or solicit charitable contributions in New York — including foundation and government grants — must register with the Charities Bureau. An organization that believes an exemption applies can claim it through the online registration process. That is a facts-based filing position, not an assumption that follows automatically from federal tax exemption.

Prepare the online-registration packet first

New York's published checklist is specific enough to use as an internal preflight. Assemble the following before starting the portal:

  • A valid email address and phone number for the Charities Bureau account.
  • The organization's EIN and federal tax classification.
  • Any previous New York registration number; search the public registry before creating a duplicate record.
  • Names, email addresses, and mailing addresses for officers, directors, trustees, and key people. New York corporations generally provide at least three unless the organization is a trust.
  • Professional fundraiser details, including name, address, and contract dates, when applicable.
  • A description of charitable purposes with at least 100 words.
  • Email addresses for the president or other authorized officer and the chief financial officer, treasurer, or other fiscal officer so the portal can route e-signatures. Trusts follow the portal's separate signature note.
  • All pages of the organizing document and bylaws or other rules document, in PDF format and within the portal's current upload limits.
  • Payment approval for the registration fee through an accepted online method.

Avoid three preventable delays

  • Search the public registry before registering so an old number or prior filing is not missed.
  • Confirm the two signers and their working email addresses before submitting the form for signature.
  • Upload complete, readable PDFs; the Charities Bureau expressly warns that missing required information delays registration.

Registration and annual reporting are different checkpoints

Initial registration establishes the organization in the Charities Bureau system. Registered organizations then have annual financial-reporting duties through the CHAR500 process when applicable. Keep the registration record, annual filing, IRS return, financial attachments, signatures, and confirmation in one New York evidence folder, but track each deadline and submission separately.

Before filing

Verify scope, registry history, exemption facts, documents, signers, professional fundraiser information, and payment authority.

During filing

Use consistent names and addresses, attach complete PDFs, route e-signatures, and save the submitted package and transaction confirmation.

After filing

Monitor requests, confirm the public registry record, record the New York registration number, and calendar annual reporting.

New York registration FAQ

Which office handles New York charity registration?

The New York Attorney General's Charities Bureau administers the registration process and public registry.

Do government or foundation grant requests matter?

The Attorney General's registration guidance expressly includes solicitation of foundation and government grants when describing organizations generally required to register.

How many signatures does the online registration use?

The published checklist calls for the president or another authorized officer and the chief financial officer, treasurer, or another person with fiscal responsibility. Trusts have a separate one-signature note.

Does registration finish New York compliance?

No. Registration is followed by annual financial-reporting duties when applicable, so calendar the CHAR500 workstream after the initial record is accepted.
